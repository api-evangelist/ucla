CNN reported on an intriguing signal detected by the LUX-ZEPLIN experiment that could offer a clue in the decades-long search for dark matter. UCLA physicist Alvine Kamaha, who helped build the detector, cautioned that one event is not enough to claim a discovery but said a confirmed detection would open “an entirely new area of particle physics.” Read more about UCLA in today’s Washington Post, The Conversation and others.
